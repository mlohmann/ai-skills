# Software engineering application review rubric

## Fit heuristic

Call this a transparent heuristic, never an actual ATS score. Start at zero; award points only for evidence supplied in the resume, verified notes, or the user's answers.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns points |
|---|---:|---|
| Required technical and functional requirements | 35 | Direct, evidenced experience with the role's essential technologies and responsibilities |
| Level, ownership, and engineering scope | 20 | Seniority, architecture, delivery, operational ownership, leadership, and decision-making appropriate to the role |
| Product, domain, and business-problem alignment | 15 | Relevant users, domain, product constraints, or business problems |
| Outcomes and evidence quality | 15 | Verified measurable results, or credible scope and outcome where a metric is unavailable |
| Communication and quick-scan clarity | 10 | Target role, progression, relevant skills, and evidence are immediately legible |
| ATS readability and terminology | 5 | Standard sections, parsable structure, consistent dates/titles, and accurate job-language alignment |

Use judgement rather than manufacturing precision. Explain which dimensions were constrained by missing evidence. Do not penalize a candidate simply for refusing to disclose metrics.

## Required audit outputs

### Requirement-to-evidence map

| Job requirement | Priority | Candidate evidence | Resume location | Status | Safe action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Status is exactly one of: `supported`, `understated`, `evidence needed`, or `genuine gap`.

### Section-and-bullet audit

For each summary claim, skill group, and experience bullet, check:

- Is it factually supported?
- Does it establish action, technical context, scope, result, or an honest reason one is unavailable?
- Is it relevant to a priority requirement?
- Is its terminology precise and naturally aligned with the job description?
- Does it create a credibility risk, ambiguity, duplication, or a need for follow-up?

Return specific edits or questions, not generic advice. If a bullet is already strong, say so and leave it unchanged.

## Two-reader review

### ATS lens

Look for parsability and findability: standard headings, a logical reading order, essential contact information in body text, a text-based file, consistent titles/dates, and real technical terms in both a skills section and supporting experience. Flag potential hazards such as tables, columns, graphics, text boxes, headers, or footers containing essential text. Do not claim certainty about any employer's configuration.

### FAANG-level hiring-manager lens

Evaluate the first scan for target-role clarity, technical depth, ownership, scope, judgment, collaboration, and outcomes. For senior roles, look for evidence of trade-offs, system or platform impact, reliability/quality, influence, and end-user or developer value. Do not impose FAANG-specific experience as a requirement unless the job description requires it.

## Question design

Ask only questions that can change a claim or decision. Good question categories include: scale; baseline and result; exact technical contribution; ownership boundaries; users or downstream consumers; reliability, accessibility, security, or performance constraints; collaborators; and why the company/team is a fit. Request confirmation before strengthening any uncertain claim.
